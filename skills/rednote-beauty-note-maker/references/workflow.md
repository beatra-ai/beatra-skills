# Beauty note workflow

## Brief card

Record the product name, supplied ingredients or features, routine step, skin or
use context, audience, tone, the experience facts the user reported and over what
period, must-keep wording, forbidden claims, and the reader action. Separate
user-supplied facts, labelled assumptions, and open questions. A product photo
shows the packaging; it is not evidence for what is inside or what it does.

Ask only when a missing fact would change a safety or efficacy claim. Everything
else can be drafted and marked as an assumption.

## Slide plan

Four `3:4` slides, one idea each: cover, texture or application, routine or
comparison, practical close. Write the cover text, hashtags, and comment starter
to match the note actually written rather than a template. Plan the slides only;
hand the approved plan to `rednote-carousel-maker` when they need to be rendered.

## The copy screen

Run this over the finished titles, body, cover text, and hashtags, on every
draft. A hit means rewrite into something specific the user supplied, never a
disclaimer appended to the claim. Report every rewrite: name the wording that was
replaced and why, so the user can decide whether to supply the fact that would
support it. Never silently drop a line the user asked for.

**Absolute superlatives.** Chinese advertising law prohibits these whether or not
they are true, and the penalty falls on the account that posts them. Screen for
at least:

```text
最   第一   顶级   国家级   销量冠军   绝对   独家   唯一   永久   根治
```

A must-keep phrase still passes through this screen. When it hits, keep the brand
or product name verbatim and rewrite only the claim around it.

**Regulated-category efficacy.** Cosmetics carry no therapeutic, curative,
medical, or functional health claim, and no promise about a skin condition.
Advertised efficacy must match the efficacy registered in the product's own
filing, so a product filed for moisturising cannot be advertised as repairing,
de-blemishing, or anti-inflammatory. A user-supplied claim does not lift this.

Ask which registered efficacy the claim corresponds to. If the user does not
know, rewrite it as their own described experience, and only for an ordinary
filed efficacy such as moisturising, cleansing, softening, texture, or wear.

China registers five efficacies separately, and a product advertised on one of
them holds its own special-cosmetic registration:

```text
祛斑美白   防晒   防脱发   染发   烫发
```

When the user confirms the product carries that registration, write the efficacy
plainly — it is the note the product exists to support. When they do not know,
the claim does not survive the personal-experience rewrite, because a
first-person account still implies the efficacy for that product. Leave it out
and say that the product's own registration is what would carry it. Any medical
or skin-condition claim, any efficacy the product neither registered nor filed,
and any claim that names no recognised efficacy category at all are left out on
the same rule.

**Outcome promises.** Promise no reach, ranking, saves, or conversion.

Keep the difference between an experience and a result visible in the copy
itself, so a reader can tell which is which without being told.

## Revision

A changed product, fact, routine step, audience, or claim is a new brief.
Preserve accepted wording, revise only the affected section, re-run the copy
screen over the revised pack, and re-mark any assumption the change invalidates.
