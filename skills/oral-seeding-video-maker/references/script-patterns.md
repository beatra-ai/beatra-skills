# Choosing the pattern

The pattern is the first decision and it governs everything after it: how many beats there are, what each one does, and how long the clip runs. Choose one. Do not blend two.

## Select by the core move

Read what the subject actually offers, then follow the shortest path to a pattern.

```text
What makes this worth watching?
├── It removes a specific, nameable frustration        → Pain-first
├── It belongs to an ordinary moment in someone's day  → Scene-embedded
├── Its result is visible and can be measured          → Unboxing test
├── The audience has to be taught something first      → Spoken authority
├── The viewer wants to be shown how                   → Build-along
└── Feeling or a relationship carries it               → Story-turn
```

When two look plausible, choose by what the first three seconds can honestly do. The hook is the only beat guaranteed to be watched, so the pattern that gives it the strongest opening wins.

## The six patterns

| Pattern | Body beats | Beat functions in order | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pain-first | 3 | pain amplified / subject enters / result shown | Functional products solving a named problem |
| Scene-embedded | 2 | everyday scene / subject folded in | High-frequency daily categories |
| Story-turn | 4 | setup / conflict / reversal / subject folded in | Emotional or relationship-led subjects |
| Spoken authority | 4 | who I am / substance delivered / recommendation / proof | Categories needing education or credibility |
| Unboxing test | 4 | opening / appearance / function tested / lived experience | New items with visible, comparable results |
| Build-along | 3 to 5 | materials / process / result | Tutorials, recipes, assembly, transformation |

Body beats are in addition to the hook and the closing ask. A pain-first clip therefore has five beats total; a four-beat pattern has six; the longest build-along has seven.

The ceiling is not a preference — it falls out of the length the clip is allowed to run.

- The clip runs 12 seconds by default and 15 seconds at the most.
- The body carries about 75 percent of it: 8 of the 12 seconds, 11 of the 15.
- Narration runs about four to five Chinese characters, or two to three English words, per second.
- A beat has to carry one complete short sentence — roughly eight Chinese characters or five English words — which is about 2 seconds at the slower rate.
- Dividing the body by that 2-second floor gives **four body beats at the 12-second default and five at the 15-second maximum**.

So no pattern exceeds five body beats, and five requires setting the target to 15 seconds. Build-along is the only pattern with a range: it reaches five body beats only at the 15-second target and stops at four like everything else at the default. A process with more stages than that merges them into an existing beat rather than becoming its own.

## Budget the beats

Hold these proportions, then round to whole seconds against the target length.

| Beat | Share of the clip | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | About 10 percent, and never under 2 seconds | It only has to buy the next beat |
| Body | About 75 percent, split by how much each beat carries | Where the substance lives |
| Closing ask | About 15 percent | Long enough to be heard, short enough not to drag |

A 12-second clip therefore runs roughly 2 seconds of hook, 8 of body, and 2 of closing; a 15-second clip runs 2, 11, and 2. Divide the body evenly enough that no beat drops under 2 seconds. Beats are contiguous: the first starts at 0, each starts where the previous ended, and the last ends at the target length.

## What each pattern needs in its hook

The hook is written last, after the body is settled, because it has to promise what the body actually delivers.

- **Pain-first** — state the frustration in the viewer's own words, before naming any subject.
- **Scene-embedded** — open inside the moment, mid-action, with no preamble.
- **Story-turn** — open at the point of tension, not at the beginning of the story.
- **Spoken authority** — open with the claim, then earn it; identity comes second, not first.
- **Unboxing test** — open on the result or the question the test will answer.
- **Build-along** — open on the finished thing, then go back to the start.

No pattern opens with a greeting, a self-introduction, or a statement of what the video is about. Those spend the only seconds that are guaranteed to be watched.

## The closing ask

One action, stated once, in the same voice as the rest. Follow, save, comment a keyword, check the link, or try the thing — pick the one the platform actually rewards for this account and say only that. Two asks halve both.

## Self-check before the shot list goes out

Score the plan out of 10 on each dimension and say what evidence justifies the score. Anything under 6 is rewritten before the user sees it.

| Dimension | What it measures |
| --- | --- |
| Hook strength | Whether the first three seconds give a reason to stay |
| Information density | Substance per second, against dead air |
| Pacing | Whether beat lengths match their jobs |
| Subject presentation | How clearly the thing being recommended is shown |
| Emotional curve | Whether tension builds and resolves rather than staying flat |
| Conversion pull | Whether the closing ask makes the next step obvious |
