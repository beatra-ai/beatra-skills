# Writing the spoken lines

The pattern gave the beats. This stage fills them with something a voice can say and a frame can show.

## Two fields, never merged

Every beat gets **on screen** and **spoken** as separate fields.

A merged field cannot be produced: the image call needs the visual with no dialogue in it, and the speech call needs the words with no stage directions in them. Writing them together means untangling them later, badly, right before a paid call.

| Field | Written for | Contains |
| --- | --- | --- |
| On screen | The image call | Subject, framing, setting, lighting, motion, any on-screen text — no dialogue |
| Spoken | The speech call | The exact words in speaking rhythm — no scene description, no stage directions |

## Budget the length before writing, not after

Natural narration runs roughly four to five Chinese characters, or two to three English words, per second. Multiply the beat's seconds by that rate and write to the result.

Writing long and trimming after synthesis costs a second paid call. Writing to budget costs nothing.

Check the total against the target before the shot list goes out. A 12-second clip carries roughly 50 to 60 Chinese characters, or 25 to 35 English words, across all beats together.

## Make it sound spoken

The line will be read aloud by a voice. Written register is audible immediately.

- Short sentences. Under fifteen characters, or under ten words, most of the time.
- One idea per sentence. A sentence carrying two ideas loses one of them in the ear.
- Mark the pauses where a person would breathe, and let the punctuation carry them.
- No written connectives — furthermore, therefore, in summary, and their equivalents. People do not say these.
- No advertising register. A line that sounds like a banner reads as a banner.
- Read it aloud once, in your head, at speaking pace. Anything you stumble over gets rewritten.

## Where the facts come from

Everything the video asserts about the subject comes from the user.

Prices, specifications, ingredients, materials, certifications, awards, results, timeframes, and promotional terms are theirs to supply. When one is missing, write the beat around it — a beat can carry a demonstration, a scene, or an observation instead of a number. Never fill the gap with a plausible-looking figure; the user is the one who answers for it.

What is safe to write without asking: what the subject visibly is and does, how it is used, who it is for, and the feeling the beat should carry.

When a missing fact would gut the beat, ask for that one fact rather than proceeding around it. One question is cheaper than a video the user cannot post.

## Screen the copy while it is still free

Run every spoken and on-screen line through three checks before anything is paid for.

**Superlatives and absolutes.** Words claiming top rank, totality, or permanence — best, first, number one, absolute, permanent, national-grade, 100% — are restricted in advertising copy in several markets, and their local-language equivalents carry the same restriction. Replace them with the specific thing that is actually true: not "the best hold", but "still in place at the end of the day" when the user has seen that.

**Efficacy claims.** Health, medical, curative, weight, whitening, and safety outcomes need substantiation the user actually holds. Without it, move the line to what the user observed rather than what the subject does. This matters most in beauty, supplement, medical device, and childcare categories, where the platform screens hardest.

**Prices.** A price stated without its unit, size, count, or condition invites a complaint. Whenever a number appears, the specification appears with it.

## Writing the on-screen field so it can become a frame

The on-screen field is going straight into an image prompt. Write it as a scene that stands alone.

Name the subject, the framing, the setting, the light, and any motion. Keep it concrete — a frame prompt cannot render "a sense of quality". Keep on-screen text short, because long text in a generated frame rarely survives legibly; when a beat depends on words appearing, put them in the note column for the user's editor instead.

Hold one look across the beats. Same palette, same light, same distance from the subject unless a beat's job is to change it. A frame set that drifts in style reads as a different video every few seconds.

## The shot list

Deliver one table the user approves before anything is paid for.

| Column | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| # | Beat order |
| Beat | Its function, from the selected pattern |
| Seconds | In and out, contiguous, ending at the target length |
| On screen | The visual, written so it can become an image prompt as-is |
| Spoken | The exact words |
| Note | On-screen text, a pace change, or a sound cue for the editor |

Mark which beats become generated frames. Not every beat needs one — the opening frame governs the finished clip, and the rest are reference material for the user's own edit. Say which is which, so the frame count in the confirmation is the count they expected.
