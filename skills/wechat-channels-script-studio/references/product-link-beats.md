# Placing the product-link beats

A WeChat Channels selling video carries a product link attached to the post. The script's job is to make the tap feel like the obvious next move rather than an interruption. That happens through three beats placed on named segments — not through a single instruction at the end.

## The three-beat chain

| Beat | Lands on | What it does | What it never does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Seed | The segment where the product first appears | Names the product plainly and once, so the viewer knows what the link would be | Sell. It is a label, not a pitch |
| Earn | The segment carrying the strongest supplied fact | Gives the reason the tap is worth making, in the same breath as the proof | Introduce a fact the fact sheet does not have |
| Ask | The closing segment | States the action in plain words and stops | Repeat, stack a second ask, or beg |

Fill the link-beat column on exactly those three rows and leave it blank elsewhere. A fourth mention dilutes the third. Keep the three on three different segments; when a short structure would land two on one row — the seed and the earn in a four-segment Offer-led script, for instance — move the seed to the segment before it.

The chain moves with the structure. In Offer-led the earn beat sits in the second segment, because the offer *is* the reason. In Plain-explainer it sits in the fourth, after the mechanism has been explained, because the tap is unearned until the viewer understands what they are buying. In Owner-voice it rides on the honest caveat, which is what makes the recommendation credible.

## Writing the ask

One action, stated once, in the same register as the rest of the script. Say what the viewer will find, not how eager you are for them to go.

- Name what is behind the link — the product, the size, the variant — so the tap has a known destination.
- Attach a reason that already appeared in the script rather than a new one.
- Let the sentence end. A trailing plea undoes the credibility the body built.

When the offer terms are supplied, the ask may state them exactly as given. When they are not, the ask points at the link and says the current offer is there. Never approximate a discount, a deadline, or a stock figure into an ask.

## Offer language and supplied facts

Every commercial specific in the chain comes from the user: price, the price it was, discount, deadline, quantity held, bundle contents, shipping terms, warranty, and return window. Reproduce them exactly as supplied — the same numbers, the same units, the same conditions.

Screen the chain before the script goes out. Being supplied is not the whole test — the first two rules below apply to the user's own wording too, because the penalty falls on the account that posts it.

- Absolute superlatives are out whether or not they are true and whoever wrote them. Replace each with the specific supplied thing underneath it. Screen for at least these and their equivalents in any language:

```text
最   第一   顶级   国家级   销量冠军   绝对   独家   唯一   100%
```

- Food, health supplements, cosmetics, and medical devices get no therapeutic, curative, or functional health claim. A supplied result or certification does not lift that ceiling; say what the product is, what it contains, and how it is used. For health supplements and medical devices, a personal account is not a way around this — attributing the outcome to someone's own stated experience is itself a restricted form for those two categories, so the line stays with what the product is rather than what it did for anyone. For food and cosmetics, a first-person account is available only when the speaker actually used the product and the user supplied that experience.
- A price with no specification attached is incomplete; pair them or drop the price.
- A price the product is said to have been has to be one it actually sold at; without that, drop the anchor and state the current price alone.
- A deadline with no date is not a deadline; either the user supplied one or the beat says the terms are on the link.
- A scarcity line with no supplied quantity is invented pressure; cut it. The same test governs the *why now* segment of an Offer-led script: it runs on a supplied limit or it comes out.
- A comparative needs both sides supplied, not one side and an assumption.

When a requested line cannot pass this screen, write the closest version the supplied facts do support, show both, and let the user decide with the difference visible.

## Rebuilding from a reference video

When the user supplies a reference video that already sold — a file, screenshots, a pasted transcript, or their own description of what happens — read its structure first and write second.

1. Segment it by second and name the function each segment performs.
2. Identify which of the six structures it follows, and where its link beats fall relative to the segments.
3. Carry the *shape* across — segment count, function order, beat placement, pacing — and replace every claim with the user's own facts.
4. Say what was carried and what was necessarily changed, so the user can see the parts that transferred.

Describe only what is visible in the reference. Its view count, sales, and conversion rate cannot be read off the video; if the user states them, attribute them to the user, and if they do not, write the teardown without them.

Do not reproduce a reference's distinctive spoken lines, on-screen text, or scripted bits verbatim. The transferable asset is the structure.
