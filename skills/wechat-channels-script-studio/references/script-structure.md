# Structuring the script

The script is the deliverable. Everything here happens in conversation and costs nothing.

## Build the fact sheet first

Before a single line is written, split what is known into three columns. The split is what keeps the script honest, and it is delivered with the script rather than kept as working notes.

| Column | What goes in it | Who owns it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supplied facts | Price, specification, ingredients, results, certifications, timeframes, offer terms, stock, awards, comparison figures | The user, only |
| Free framing | What the product is, who it is for, when it gets used, what using this kind of thing is like, why the category matters | The writing |
| Named gaps | Every supplied fact the structure wants and does not have | Listed by name, not silently skipped |

A gap is written around, not filled. If the offer terms are unknown, the closing beat says the offer is on the link rather than naming a discount. If the result is unknown, the demonstration beat shows the use rather than claiming an outcome. Deliver the gap list with the script and name the smallest input that closes each one.

First person is not free framing. An ownership period, a personal result, a before-and-after, and the honest caveat an Owner-voice script rests on are all claims about the speaker's own experience: write one only from what the user stated, and otherwise let the line describe the use rather than testify to it.

A reference video is read the same way. Its segmentation, beat order, and the move each beat makes are visible and may be described. Its view count, sales, and conversion rate are not visible from the video, so treat them as the user's to state.

## Choose the structure

WeChat Channels distribution runs through the social graph before it runs through interest matching. A viewer's likes surface a post to people who know them, so a script earns reach by being worth passing on — not only by being watched to the end. That pushes the opening away from shock and toward recognition: the first line should make the right viewer think *this is about me*, or *someone I know needs this*.

Take the structure from the reference video when one was given. Otherwise select one.

```text
What carries this product?
├── It fixes something the viewer already complains about   → Problem-recognition
├── It belongs to a routine moment                          → Day-in-the-life
├── Its difference is visible side by side                  → Comparison
├── The category needs explaining before it can be sold     → Plain-explainer
├── The value is in who is recommending it                  → Owner-voice
└── The offer itself is the news                            → Offer-led
```

| Structure | Segments | Segment functions in order | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Problem-recognition | 5 | recognition / cost of the problem / product enters / proof / ask | Functional goods with a nameable frustration |
| Day-in-the-life | 4 | scene / product folded in / why it stuck / ask | High-frequency household and personal categories |
| Comparison | 5 | the two options / what changes / the visible difference / who it suits / ask | Upgrades and replacements |
| Plain-explainer | 5 | the question / the short answer / why it works / what to buy / ask | Health, care, tools, anything mis-sold |
| Owner-voice | 4 | who is speaking / what they use and why / the honest caveat / ask | Shop owners, makers, local businesses, professional accounts |
| Offer-led | 4 | the offer stated / what it applies to / why now / ask | Real promotions with real terms supplied |

Do not blend two structures. When two look plausible, choose by what the first three seconds can honestly say with the facts on hand.

## Time the segments

Default target is 45 seconds. Hold these proportions and round to whole seconds.

| Segment | Share | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening | About 3 seconds, never less than 2 | It only has to buy the second segment |
| Body | Everything between the opening and the ask, split by what each segment carries | Where the substance lives |
| Ask | About 15 percent | Long enough to be heard, short enough not to drag |

Narration runs about four to five Chinese characters, or two to three English words, per second at a natural reading pace. Write to that budget rather than trimming afterwards: a segment that needs nine seconds of speech does not fit in six. Segments are contiguous — the first starts at 0, each starts where the previous ended, and the last ends at the target.

A target past 90 seconds is confirmed with the user rather than chosen alone. Longer scripts do not fail on length; they fail because the single idea splits.

## Write the segment table

One row per segment, five columns, in this order.

| Segment | In–out | On screen | Spoken line | Link beat |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

- **On screen** is what the camera sees and what the person does — framed so it can be shot on a phone, alone, without a crew.
- **Spoken line** is the exact words, not a description of them. It is the column a person reads while filming.
- **Link beat** is filled only on the segments that carry one, per [placing the product-link beats](product-link-beats.md).

Then write the narration a second time as continuous prose, with no table around it, punctuated for breath. That version is what gets read aloud or voiced.

## Rules the spoken lines follow

- Speak the way the account already speaks. Match register, sentence length, and vocabulary to whatever sample the user gave; when none was given, write plain, warm, unhurried speech and say that was the choice.
- One idea per script. A second selling point that needs its own setup belongs in a second video.
- No greeting, no self-introduction, and no statement of what the video is about in the opening. Those spend the only seconds guaranteed to be watched.
- Every number, claim, and comparative traces to a supplied fact. If it does not, rewrite the line so it does not need one.
- Superlatives and regulated-category efficacy claims come out at the draft stage, when the wording is still free to change — including ones the user supplied, per the screen in [placing the product-link beats](product-link-beats.md).
- One ask, stated once, in the same voice as the rest.

## Score the draft

Score each dimension out of 10 and say what in the draft justifies the score. Anything under 6 is rewritten before the user sees it. Name the weakest dimension explicitly in the delivery.

| Dimension | What it measures |
| --- | --- |
| Opening pull | Whether the first three seconds give the right viewer a reason to stay |
| Claim sourcing | Whether every figure and comparative traces to a supplied fact |
| One-idea focus | Whether the script sells one thing, not three |
| Spoken rhythm | Whether the lines read aloud at pace without stumbling |
| Link-beat earning | Whether the tap is motivated by the segment before it |
| Pass-on value | Whether a viewer would send this to one specific person |
