# Narration script copywriting

`script.txt` holds only the spoken words. Scene notes, timecodes, and scene
numbers live in `DESIGN.md`.

The voice is **one person talking to one person, like a colleague at the next
desk**. Structure follows the social hook arc; sentences follow the
plain-language rules. The test: read it aloud once; anything you stumble on
gets rewritten. Plain language = speakable, understandable on one pass, no
rewinding.

**Viewers are not here to learn; they are here to be hooked.** Correct
content with a first three seconds that loses them saves nothing.

## Speakable

Periods and question marks are the pauses. Write how proper nouns, numbers,
and English terms are read, into the line itself (for example, reading notes
like the one fenced below).

Target roughly **200 characters per minute**. Over budget: cut examples
first, raise speed second.

One narration block maps to one scene: `# s1` maps to `#s1` in the HTML.
Inside a scene, **one line is one breath and one caption**. Break at thought
groups, around 18 characters per line; longer lines break at commas. Never
stack a whole paragraph into one line. Every sentence must contain a noun you
can draw on screen; the storyboard's on-screen words are pulled from these
nouns (see [visual rules](visual-rules.md)).

```text
# s1
你跟聊天模型说话，它答完就停。
# s2
给它一个目标，它拆步骤、调工具、看结果。
# s3
Agent 就是模型、工具，加上这个循环。
```

## Diagnose first

Before polishing, ask three questions about the skeleton; any "yes" means
restructure first:

1. **Is the opening a hook or a self-introduction?** "I was scrolling
   recently and noticed…" and "today let me explain" are not openings; they
   are reasons to swipe away.
2. **Is the structure a conflict or a lecture?** Naming the topic first
   ("what is X") and reaching the example later is teaching. The viewer owes
   you no patience.
3. **Why does anyone stay?** Only three reasons: *it is about me / it
   contradicts me / it makes me curious*. If none holds, reorder.

## Structure

```text
hook (0–3s) → state the conflict → evidence (2–3 contrast scenes) →
landing (one view, one paragraph) → closing action
```

- **Hook.** The first sentence opens the gap with a conflict from inside the
  material. Never open with a definition. Contrast first: two things that
  look identical but end up worlds apart.
- **State the conflict.** One sentence on what confusing them costs, so it
  feels personal.
- **Evidence.** One concrete scenario per block, each written as
  "you + action + result". Do not explain the concept. Every 10–15 seconds
  needs new information or a small hook (a question or a one-line pattern).
  One sharp contrast beats a forced triple.
- **Landing.** The view — or the product — gets at most one paragraph, in
  "if you want X, go to Y" shape. No front-loaded-objective phrasing.
- **Closing.** One concrete action (what to watch next, who to send this to).
  No "thanks for watching", no like-and-follow, no "comment 1".

Iron rule: **never throw away existing material; reorder beats rewriting.**
A flat script is usually entirely correct with its sharpest conflict buried
in paragraph four. Move the conflict to sentence one.

Never write a purchase ad (pain → solution → buy). No definition openings,
no "first we will look at… then…" previews.

## Six hook styles

Pick one. Material with built-in conflict > pain > counter-intuitive.
Contrast is strongest and needs no prior knowledge.

| Style | What it does |
|---|---|
| Contrast | Two things that look identical, opposite outcomes |
| Pain | Name the annoyance they are suffering right now |
| Counter-intuitive | Challenge something they already believe |
| Benefit | Say plainly what they walk away with; never invent numbers or timelines |
| Question | The question itself opens the gap |
| Identity | Name the crowd; no slogan personas |

Platforms change only the first sentence's flavor, never the structure:
Douyin wants the contrast harsh and the hook inside one second; Xiaohongshu
front-loads the pain and sounds like teaching a method; WeChat Channels aims
at workplace identity.

## Rewrite pass

1. Read the whole draft; locate problems with the three diagnostic
   questions.
2. Pick the hook style; find the sharpest contrast already in the material.
3. Reorder: move the conflict scene to the top; delete or demote the
   self-introduction.
4. Give each evidence block a closing micro-hook.
5. Rewrite the landing in plain words: "if you want to do X, use Y", with a
   subject in every sentence.
6. Write the closing action.
7. Read once against the six plain-language rules; fix stumbles. Never ask
   for a "more human" wholesale rewrite.

## Plain language

### 1. A subject in every sentence

Telegraphic sentences with dropped subjects get restored. Ask per sentence:
who does this, who receives it.

### 2. No long-distance references

Spoken audio is linear; listeners cannot scroll back for "this" or "that
kind". Name the noun again.

### 3. No written-register filler

Bookish framing needs translation in the ear, and translation stumbles.
Replace scene-setting phrases with concrete moments ("when you are writing
the weekly report"). Terms are either dropped or immediately followed by an
example.

Also out: greeting scaffolding, "in summary", "essentially", "studies show",
growth-hacker jargon.

### 4. Hook with the example; name the term last

The opening conflict must be one concrete, visible thing (a finished deck, a
cut clip). The main example carries the middle of the film. Side cases do
not open a second full contrast. The newsy name lands after the evidence.

### 5. Delete sentences that do no work

Repetitions, restatements, rhythm filler: if deleting it changes nothing,
delete it. Short beats watery.

### 6. Comparisons on one scale

"A pile of words" versus "a thing you can open" only compares when both
sides are at the same granularity. One concrete, one abstract: dead
comparison.

## Pre-delivery checklist

- [ ] The first sentence stops a thumb within three seconds
- [ ] The opening is not self-introduction
- [ ] A conflict, contrast, or pain lands inside the first 30 seconds
- [ ] Every 10–15 seconds brings a new point or micro-hook
- [ ] Every sentence has a subject; no front-loaded objects, no written
      filler
- [ ] No "…is exactly this" style dangling references
- [ ] The view or product landing is at most one paragraph
- [ ] The ending is a concrete action, not thanks
- [ ] Comparison sentences sit on one scale
- [ ] Read aloud once, no stumbles
- [ ] Deleting any single sentence would lose meaning

Never invent prices, benchmark numbers, headcounts, or timelines. Never
claim unrun work as run. Benefit promises state a judgment, not a fake
result.
