# Creative brief and style

Build a coherent production direction rather than a bag of keywords.

## Brief template

Write the internal brief in this order:

1. **Use and listener** — single, ad BGM, game loop, film cue, social clip,
   meditation, children's song, karaoke, demo, or another use.
2. **Core feeling and arc** — where the listener begins, the emotional turn,
   and where the music should land.
3. **Genre and era** — one primary family plus at most two compatible
   influences.
4. **Tempo and groove** — BPM only when the user needs it; otherwise describe
   slow/medium/fast and straight, swung, syncopated, halftime, or four-on-floor.
5. **Harmony and melody** — bright/dark, stable/restless, memorable/restrained,
   diatonic/modal/jazzy, narrow/wide vocal range.
6. **Instrumentation and texture** — lead, rhythm section, acoustic/electronic
   palette, density, and featured moments.
7. **Structure and dynamics** — opening, build, peak, release, ending, and
   approximate section proportions.
8. **Voice and language** — language, region, script, register, intimacy,
   power, gender preference, solo/duet/group, and any bilingual or mixed
   section plan.
9. **Mix and exclusions** — dry/intimate or wide/cinematic, vintage/modern,
   and the most important unwanted traits.

## Choosing a direction

Offer no more than three options, each with a clear creative thesis. A useful
option changes the listener experience, not merely the adjective list.

Examples:

- **Intimate confession** — sparse piano, close vocal, restrained drums, a
  chorus that opens through harmony rather than volume.
- **City-night momentum** — synth-pop pulse, bright bass movement, clipped
  verses, wide hook, polished modern mix.
- **Cinematic ascent** — ambient opening, ostinato growth, live strings and
  percussion, delayed peak, resolved final chord.

Choose based on the purpose. Background music needs space for dialogue and
visuals; a chorus-led single needs a strong melodic and lyrical hook; karaoke
needs breathable phrases and a practical vocal range.

For BGM, requested duration and “leave room for narration” are creative
directions rather than dedicated duration or automatic mixing controls. Shape
the energy curve and reduce lead density, sharp percussion, and busy midrange,
but tell the user that exact timing, a sample-perfect loop, dialogue sidechain,
or final frequency balance may require editing and mixing after generation.

## Useful style vocabulary

Combine compatible terms from these groups:

- Pop: contemporary pop, synth-pop, dream pop, indie pop, city pop, dance-pop,
  acoustic pop, power ballad.
- Electronic: ambient, downtempo, lo-fi, chillhop, house, deep house, future
  bass, synthwave, drum and bass, cinematic electronica.
- Rock: indie rock, alternative rock, pop rock, soft rock, post-rock,
  psychedelic rock, punk energy, arena chorus.
- R&B and soul: contemporary R&B, neo-soul, gospel harmony, funk groove,
  smooth soul, quiet storm.
- Hip-hop: boom bap, trap, melodic rap, jazz rap, lo-fi hip-hop, drill energy.
- Acoustic and roots: folk, singer-songwriter, country pop, blues, bossa nova,
  Latin acoustic, flamenco color, fingerstyle guitar, West African guitar
  interplay, acoustic ensemble.
- Orchestral and screen: cinematic score, chamber strings, epic trailer,
  suspense underscore, romantic piano, fantasy, documentary, game soundtrack.
- Global and regional colors: Chinese instruments, guzheng, pipa, erhu,
  bamboo flute, Cantonese pop, Mandarin ballad, Japanese city pop, anime
  opening, Afrobeats pulse, amapiano log-drum groove, Latin pop, reggaeton,
  Arabic strings, South Asian percussion, Nordic folk texture, traditional
  percussion. Use specific musical elements with context; do not reduce a
  culture to a stereotype or combine regions as interchangeable decoration.
- Functional: café BGM, meditation, sleep, study, podcast intro, advertising
  jingle, fashion film, product launch, wedding, children's music, sports
  highlight, loop-friendly game cue.

Use production words when helpful: warm tape texture, sidechained pads, crisp
drums, muted bass, close-miked vocal, airy backing harmonies, call-and-response,
key change, breakdown, instrumental interlude, clean radio mix.

## Prompt construction

Write a compact, ordered production note:

`purpose + primary genre + emotional arc + tempo/groove + key instruments +
structure/dynamics + vocal/language + mix + exclusions`

Do not stuff mutually conflicting genres into one prompt. Put negative
requirements such as “no aggressive drums, no spoken intro” into Suno
`negative_tags` when using custom controls. Language and duet instructions are
soft guidance and should also be reinforced by the lyrics themselves.
