# Screening the copy

Read every line — caption, title, on-screen text, tags, and any script — against the categories below. Return findings as a table, and never return a flag without a replacement.

| Column | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| Where | Caption, title, on-screen text, tag, or script line |
| Phrase | The exact wording, quoted |
| Why | Which rule it touches, in one clause — and a note when the replacement changes the substance of the claim rather than only its wording, so the user can weigh that row separately |
| Replacement | Wording that keeps the selling intent and does not carry the exposure |
| Severity | Rewrite before publishing, or worth a second look |

A finding the user cannot act on in five seconds is a finding they will ignore.

## Superlatives and absolutes

Claims of top rank, totality, or permanence are restricted in advertising copy across several markets, and their local-language equivalents carry the same restriction. This is the highest-volume category by far, and the easiest to fix.

Screen for claims of being first or best, of totality or completeness, of permanence or being irreversible, of official or national standing, and of unqualified quantities like every, all, or a full hundred percent.

The replacement is almost always the specific true thing underneath the superlative. "The best hold on the market" becomes "still in place at the end of the day" when that is what the user has actually observed. The specific version usually converts better anyway, because it is concrete.

Watch for the disguised forms: a superlative inside a quotation, inside a customer testimonial, in a tag, or rendered as on-screen text in a cover. The exposure does not change because the wording moved location.

## Regulated-category claims

Some categories carry claim rules regardless of how the post is phrased. Identify the category first, then apply its ceiling.

| Category | What needs substantiation the user holds |
| --- | --- |
| Beauty and skincare | Whitening, anti-ageing, repair, acne treatment, allergy safety |
| Supplements and food | Any health outcome, disease reference, weight effect, curative language |
| Medical and devices | Diagnosis, treatment, cure, efficacy rate, clinical framing |
| Childcare | Development, nutrition, safety guarantees |
| Finance | Returns, guarantees, risk-free framing, past-performance implication |
| Education | Pass rates, admission outcomes, employment results |

When substantiation is not in hand, the reliable move is to shift the line from what the product does to what the user observed or what the experience was. That keeps the post publishable and keeps the claim honest.

## Prices, offers, and comparisons

- Every price carries its unit, size, count, or condition.
- A struck-through original price has to be a price the item actually sold at.
- A limited offer states what limits it — a real end time or a real quantity.
- A comparison to a named competitor needs evidence the user holds. Without it, compare to the situation before the product instead.
- A discount expressed as a fraction of an unstated base is not a claim a reader can check, and reads as one.

## Platform-specific wording

Beyond advertising rules, each platform restricts wording that routes traffic off it, that promises engagement in exchange for action, or that names a competing platform. Screen for off-platform contact details, guaranteed-return-for-comment phrasing, and instructions that only make sense on another app.

Report these separately from the advertising findings — they carry a different consequence, usually reduced distribution rather than a takedown, and users triage them differently.

## Writing a replacement that survives

A replacement that guts the line will be rejected and the original will ship. Three rules keep replacements usable.

1. **Keep the intent.** If the line was there to create urgency, the replacement creates urgency by another means.
2. **Keep the register.** A conversational line stays conversational. A formal claim replaced with slang reads as an edit, and the user will notice before their audience does.
3. **Keep the length.** On-screen text and titles have space constraints; a replacement twice as long does not fit the cover it was written for.

When no replacement can carry the whole intent, still give one: name what the line is trying to do, say which part of that the replacement drops, and offer the nearest defensible wording. Every flagged phrase leaves with something the user can put in its place, even when that something trades away part of the original claim.

## What this read is

This is a structured read of the wording against documented restrictions, current as of what is written here. It tells the user what their copy exposes them to. It does not clear the post — that judgment stays with them, and where a line matters commercially they should have it reviewed by whoever answers for it.

Say that once, at the end of the compliance read, and do not repeat it in every finding.
