# Reading it as the audience

The compliance read says what the copy exposes. This says whether it works.

## Build the profiles from the copy

Five reader profiles by default, constructed from who the copy is evidently written for plus anything the user supplied about their audience.

Each profile needs four attributes and nothing more: life stage, what they came to the platform for, what they already believe about this category, and what would make them scroll past. More attributes produce more convincing profiles without producing better reads.

Spread the profiles across the realistic range rather than clustering on the ideal buyer. A set that includes the sceptic, the price-sensitive reader, and the person who is not sure this is for them will find more than five variations of the target customer.

When the user names their audience, use it. When they name a segment the copy is clearly not written for, say so — that mismatch is itself the most valuable finding in the read.

## Run the read

For each profile, work through the post in order and report:

| Field | What goes in it |
| --- | --- |
| First reaction | What they think at the end of the first line |
| Where they stop | The exact line they scroll past on, quoted — or that they finish |
| Why | What that line did wrong for this profile specifically |
| What would land | The one change that keeps this profile reading |
| Would they act | Against the post's actual ask, not in general |

The quoted line is the whole point. "This feels a bit salesy" is not actionable. "They leave at *limited stock, order now* because nothing before it gave them a reason to want it" is.

## Write the reactions as the reader, not about the reader

A reaction written in the reader's own register is worth several written in analyst language. Short, specific, unflattering where it should be.

Do not soften. A read where all five profiles are mildly positive has told the user nothing and cost them the chance to fix it before publishing.

## Score the reach

Three scores out of 10, each with the line or structural feature that justifies it. A score without evidence is a number.

| Score | What it measures | Evidence looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | Whether the opening earns the next line | The opening line, quoted, and what it promises |
| Hold | Whether the middle keeps someone who started | Where the substance actually lands |
| Share | Whether anyone has a reason to pass it on | What a sharer would say about it |

Share is the one most copy fails. A post can be well written, compliant, and completely unshareable — nothing in it makes the sharer look informed, useful, or interesting to whoever they send it to. When that is the diagnosis, say it, because it is fixable and the user usually has not considered it.

## Assemble the corrected copy

The three reads produce changes. Fold them into one clean version, and show the change list beside it.

| Column | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| Line | The original, quoted |
| Changed to | The new wording |
| Why | Compliance finding, audience finding, or reach finding |

The user rejects any of them individually. That is why the list exists: a corrected version they cannot audit is a version they will not trust enough to publish.

Deliver the corrected copy as one block they can copy without editing, with the cover wording called out separately if there is any — that line is the one that goes into the render, and it is the one most worth getting right.

## What this read is

Reader profiles are constructed from the copy and the user's own description of their audience. This is a structured read, not measured research with real readers. Say that once, when the audience read is delivered, and let the specificity of the findings carry the rest.
