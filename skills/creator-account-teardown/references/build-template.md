# Building your own account

The diagnosis is the input. This is the output the user actually runs: a positioning line, a bio, a content matrix, a hook formula, an opening plan, and one post written well enough to produce.

## Positioning line

One sentence, in this shape: for whom, what they get, and why from you.

It has to survive three tests. It is specific enough that a competitor could not paste it onto their own account. It is true — every part of it traces to something the user actually told you about themselves. And it is narrower than the analysed account's, because entering a board someone else already owns on the same width is the one move that reliably fails.

Offer one line, plus one deliberately narrower alternative, and say what each costs and buys. Do not ask the user to choose between five.

## Bio

Write it to the platform's shape, ready to paste. It carries three things: who this is for, what they get, and one reason to believe — a credential, a number the user supplied, or a specific proof. Then whatever the platform allows as a next step.

Nothing in the bio may be a fact the user did not supply.

## Content matrix

Three to five pillars. More than five is not a matrix, it is a lack of positioning.

For each pillar, fix:

- **The pillar and its job.** Reach, trust, or conversion. An account of only reach pillars grows and sells nothing; an account of only conversion pillars sells to nobody.
- **The formats it runs in.** Two or three, taken from formats the diagnosis showed working and the user can actually shoot.
- **The share.** How many posts out of every ten.
- **The cadence.** Posts per week, set against what the user can genuinely sustain rather than what the analysed account does.

Then state the whole matrix as one line the user can hold in their head: *three of every ten posts are X, five are Y, two are Z.*

Where the matrix departs from the analysed account's, say why. Usually it is the weak point from the diagnosis, deliberately taken.

## The hook formula

This is the most reusable thing in the whole deliverable, and it is the piece a report leaves out.

Write the pattern the diagnosis found as a fill-in template the user can run on a new subject — the move, the slot, and the constraint. Then write three worked examples on three different subjects from the user's own pillars, so the template is proven rather than asserted.

Say where it came from: which posts, how many, and that it is a read of a pattern rather than a measured rule. A formula the user runs and adjusts is worth more than a claim about why it works.

## The opening plan

Ten posts, in order, each with its pillar, its format, its working title or hook line, and one line on what it is for. Order matters more than the individual posts:

- The first three establish the positioning — after them, a stranger should be able to state what this account is about.
- The next four run the pillars at their intended shares so the pattern becomes visible.
- Posts eight to ten include the account's first conversion move, whatever that is for the user.

Mark which posts need something the user does not yet have — footage, a product shot, a guest, a result — so a missing asset shows up now rather than on shooting day.

## The first post

Take the strongest post from the opening plan and write it out in full.

- **Cover wording.** Six to twelve characters in Chinese, or a short phrase in English. It carries the hook, not the topic. Write the words exactly as they should appear.
- **Cover art direction.** Subject, framing, setting, lighting, and mood, written as a scene rather than a description of a design.
- **Caption.** In the platform's voice, opening on the hook.
- **Hashtags.** As many as the platform rewards, ordered broad to narrow.
- **Script.** Beat by beat, with two separate fields per beat: what is on screen, and what is said. A merged field cannot be shot.

Budget the spoken fields before anything is produced. Total them at roughly four to five Chinese characters or two to three English words per second and compare against the length the format wants. Cutting a script while it is still free text costs nothing; cutting it after it has been voiced costs a call.

## Screening the copy before it is produced

Read the whole post back once:

- Does every claim about the user's product, results, or credentials trace to something they supplied? Cut or rewrite anything that does not.
- Does any line reproduce the analysed account's wording rather than its structure? Rewrite it.
- Would the hook still work if the subject were swapped? If not, it is a topic, not a hook.
- Does the cover wording and the opening line say the same thing twice? Give them different jobs.

Then show the whole template and the first post together, and get the post approved before anything is produced.
