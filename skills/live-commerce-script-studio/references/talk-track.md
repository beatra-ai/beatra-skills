# Writing the talk track

The schedule says when. This says what the host actually says.

## Write lines to be read, not instructions to be interpreted

The single most common failure in a live script is a cell that says "introduce the selling points warmly". On air, at hour three, that cell is useless. Write the sentence.

Every cell that reaches the host is a line they can read at speaking pace without translating it first. Directions belong in the cue column, not in the spoken column.

## Seven steps per featured product

Each featured product gets its own block, in this order.

| Step | What it does | What the line must contain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Problem | Name the frustration in the viewer's words | A situation, not a product |
| 2. Introduce | Bring the product in as the answer | What it is, in one sentence |
| 3. Selling points | Feature, what it does, what it means, evidence | Four beats per point, two or three points maximum |
| 4. Trust | Why believe it | Merchant-supplied proof only |
| 5. Price anchor | Regular price, then live price | Both numbers, with the specification |
| 6. Urgency | Why now | Real stock or real time, stated as fact |
| 7. Questions | The three things the room always asks | Sizing, timing, aftercare, or the category equivalent |

Two or three selling points is the ceiling. A fourth dilutes the first three, and a room that hears five remembers none.

Step 5 always states the regular price before the live price. The live price alone has no meaning; the gap is what carries the decision.

## Twelve categories of ready-to-read lines

The library is what the host falls back on when the track runs out and the room is still there. Three to five lines per category, each readable as written.

| Category | When it is used |
| --- | --- |
| Opening | Session start and each rotation start |
| Welcome | New arrivals mid-slot |
| Holding | Keeping the room through a slow stretch |
| Interaction | Prompting comments and reactions |
| Question response | Answering the recurring asks |
| Objection | Price, doubt, comparison to alternatives |
| Demonstration | Narrating what the camera is showing |
| Trust | Restating supplied proof without overclaiming |
| Offer restate | Repeating the numbers for late arrivals |
| Urgency | Stock and time pressure |
| Countdown | The last thirty seconds before a link closes |
| Closing | Session end, next-session hook, follow ask |

Vary the wording inside each category. A host repeating one line verbatim every four minutes sounds automated, and the room hears it.

## Three kinds of urgency

Urgency lines are where scripts most often drift into claims the merchant cannot support. Choose the type from what is actually true.

| Type | Built from | Line contains |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Price | The gap between regular and live price, and when it ends | Both figures and the real end time |
| Stock | Remaining quantity | The real number, updated as it moves |
| Problem | The cost of not solving it | The consequence, never a health outcome |

Each type works on a different lever — a closing window, a shrinking quantity, a persisting problem. Rotating between them across a slot is more effective than repeating one, and it stops the room from tuning out a single note.

Never invent a number to create pressure. A stock figure that does not move as it is announced is visible to the room and costs more credibility than the urgency buys.

## Screen every line before anything is produced

**Superlatives and absolutes.** Words claiming top rank, totality, or permanence are restricted in advertising copy in several markets, whether or not the claim is true, and their local-language equivalents carry the same restriction. Live sessions are transcribed and reviewed, so a spoken superlative carries the same exposure as a written one. Screen for at least these, and for the same claims in any other language the session is read in:

```text
最   第一   顶级   国家级   销量冠军   绝对   独家   唯一   永久   万能   史上最低
```

Do not screen by substring. The ranking word for "first" also begins ordinary
time and sequence phrases that claim no rank at all:

```text
✓ 第一天     ← 时间序数，保留
✓ 第一次用   ← 次序，保留
✗ 全网第一   ← 排名主张，删除
```

Cutting the first two destroys compliant copy. Read what the phrase asserts.

Replace each with the specific thing the merchant has actually supplied. The pattern is always the same — swap the ranking for the fact that earned it:

| Instead of | Say |
| --- | --- |
| a top-rank claim about sales | the number the merchant supplied, with its period |
| a totality claim about who it suits | the two or three cases it was tested on |
| an "only one on the market" claim | the one feature no competitor line was shown to have |
| a permanence claim about the effect | how long the merchant's own test observed it holding |
| a "lowest ever" price claim | the regular price and the live price, both with the specification |

When nothing has been supplied, the line does not get softened — it comes out, and the merchant is told which line and why.

**Efficacy claims.** Health, medical, curative, weight, whitening, and safety outcomes need substantiation the merchant actually holds. Without it, move the line to what the product does rather than what it achieves — not "clears acne" but "the merchant's own eight-week panel reported less redness", once they confirm it.

Four categories are screened hardest, and each has a line that decides it:

| Category | The line that decides it |
| --- | --- |
| Beauty and skincare | Does the claim describe an appearance the viewer can see, or a change inside the skin? The second needs substantiation. |
| Supplements and food | Is a health outcome named at all? Naming one at all requires the merchant's registration document. |
| Medical devices | Is the registration number on the listing? Without it the product cannot be described by what it treats. |
| Childcare | Is a safety outcome asserted? Safety is a claim, not a feature, and needs the test report. |

**Prices.** Every price appears with its unit, size, or count. A regular price used as an anchor has to be a price the product actually sold at.

**Held stock.** Every hold states its release time and quantity.

**Comparisons.** A comparison to a named competitor needs merchant-supplied evidence. Without it, compare to the situation before the product instead.

Deliver the compliance pass as its own list: what was checked, what was changed and to what, and what still needs the merchant's confirmation before air.

## The optional verbatim script

The four written pieces are the default delivery. A full word-for-word script for a whole session is not, because stock figures move during the session, a four-hour verbatim reads as recitation, and no host reads twenty thousand words without losing their place.

Produce one anyway for a specific product when the merchant asks and the situation warrants it: a first-time host who needs a floor to stand on, a brand stream where several hosts must say the same thing, or a high-consideration or regulated category where improvisation is the risk. Write it segmented by the six stages, with the moving figures left as marked slots the floor manager fills in.
