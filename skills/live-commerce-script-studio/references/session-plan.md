# Planning the session

The schedule is the piece everyone in the room works from. It has to be specific enough that a floor manager can call the next move without asking.

## Set the frame first

Five decisions, made once, before anything is written.

| Decision | Default when unstated | What it changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Platform | The one the merchant already sells on | Wording restrictions and what the closing ask can say |
| Goal | Read from the lineup | Whether the session leans to reach or to conversion |
| Length | Four hours | Rotation count and how many times each product returns |
| On-air roles | Host plus floor manager | Whether the track has a second voice and a cue column |
| Format | Creator-led selling | How much of the session is brand narrative |

A session past six hours is split rather than stretched — attention and the host both degrade, and the second half converts worse than a fresh session would. A session under an hour drops to a single rotation and a trimmed line library.

## Three phases across the whole session

The session as a whole has a shape, independent of any single product.

| Phase | Share | What it is for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening | First 15 to 20 percent | Gather an audience, set the offer frame, warm the room before the strongest product |
| Selling | Middle 65 to 70 percent | Rotations through the lineup, strongest products placed where traffic peaks |
| Closing | Last 15 percent | Last call on the remaining stock, next-session hook, follow ask |

Traffic on most platforms peaks partway into the selling phase rather than at the start. Place the strongest product at that peak, not first — opening with it spends it on the smallest audience of the session.

## Six stages inside every product slot

Each product's slot runs the same chain. This is the micro-structure inside the macro schedule.

```text
gather → hold → prove → persuade → press → close
```

- **Gather** — a reason for new arrivals to stay through the next two minutes.
- **Hold** — the problem or the scene the product belongs to.
- **Prove** — demonstration, comparison, or evidence the merchant supplied.
- **Persuade** — the offer framed against the regular price.
- **Press** — the urgency, built from real stock and real time.
- **Close** — the link is up, the action is stated once, the count is called.

A slot missing **prove** converts on price alone and trains the room to wait for discounts. A slot missing **press** produces interest without orders.

## Order the lineup

Sequence by role, not by margin.

| Role | Where it goes | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Warm-up | Opening phase | Low-price, fast-decision item that starts the buying habit |
| Anchor | At the traffic peak | The strongest product, given the longest slot |
| Volume | Between anchors | The steady sellers that carry the middle |
| Return | Last third | A repeat of the anchor for viewers who arrived late |

Every product returns at least once in a session over two hours. Viewers arrive continuously, and a product shown once is a product most of the audience never saw.

## The schedule table

One row per slot. Minute-level, contiguous, covering the whole session.

| Column | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| Start / end | Clock minutes from session start, contiguous |
| Phase | Opening, selling, or closing |
| Product | Name, or the activity when no product is featured |
| Slot role | Warm-up, anchor, volume, or return |
| Stage | Which of the six stages this row covers |
| Host action | What the host is doing and saying at this point |
| Floor cue | Link up, stock update, pinned comment, countdown |
| Offer state | Regular price, live price, and remaining stock when the slot's urgency runs on quantity |
| Note | Anything the room needs to know before it happens |

Contiguity matters more than precision. A schedule with gaps is a schedule the room stops following twenty minutes in.

## Holding stock back

Holding stock back to build pressure is common and is regulated on several platforms. When the schedule uses it, every held slot states the release time and the quantity that will be released, and the host announces both. A hold with no stated release is the version that draws penalties, and it also breaks trust with a room that waited.
