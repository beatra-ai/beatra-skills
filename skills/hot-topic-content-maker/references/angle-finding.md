# Finding the angle

A topic is not content. The angle is what turns someone else's trend into something this account can post.

## Read the topic first

Before looking for angles, establish four things about the topic. Ask only for what is genuinely missing, and only once — speed is the whole point of this route.

| What | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| What actually happened | The angle has to be about the real thing, not a headline |
| Why people are talking | The emotion driving the conversation is what an angle attaches to |
| How long it has left | A topic peaking today is worth a same-day post; one already fading is not — this comes from the user or from an attributed lookup, never from your own read; see *Facts about real events* |
| Who is talking | Whether the audience overlaps with this account at all — same source rule |

When the user pasted a list rather than one topic, rank it by overlap with the account and say which one you are working from and why. A weaker topic with real overlap beats the biggest topic on the board with none.

## Six ways in

Most workable angles are one of these. Generate across the types rather than three variations of one.

| Angle | The move | Works when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shared experience | The account has lived the thing the topic is about | The topic is everyday |
| Expert read | The account explains what others are guessing at | The account has real standing |
| Practical use | The topic creates a concrete need this account serves | The link is functional |
| Contrarian | The account disagrees with the obvious take, with reasons | The consensus is lazy |
| Behind the scenes | The topic surfaces something this account does daily | The account has access others do not |
| Format borrow | The topic's format is reused, its content replaced | The format is what spread, not the subject |

Format borrow is the one people forget. When a sound, a structure, or a visual template is what actually spread, the topic itself may not need to appear at all.

## The connection has to be stated

Every angle carries one sentence naming what links the topic to this account. If that sentence needs a qualifier — "loosely", "in a way", "if you think about it" — the angle is a stretch, and stretched angles read as opportunism.

An account with no honest connection to a topic is better served by the next topic. Say so and offer what would connect instead.

## Rank by what it can actually do

Offer three angles, ordered. For each, state:

- the connection in one sentence;
- what the post would say;
- who it reaches that the account's usual content does not;
- how quickly it has to go out to still matter;
- anything about it the user should decide on rather than have decided for them.

Do not pad to three. Two strong angles and an honest note that the third would be a stretch is more useful than three of equal-looking quality.

## The risk read

Some topics attach an account to something the user, not the workflow, should decide about. Name it plainly, once, beside the angle it applies to, then let the user choose.

Read for these:

- **Someone's loss.** Accidents, deaths, disasters, and illness. Commercial association here reliably backfires, and the account carries it long after the topic fades.
- **A live dispute.** An unresolved controversy where taking a side is a positioning decision with consequences beyond this post.
- **Someone else's name.** A person, brand, or company being criticised. Using it to sell borrows a fight the account may not want.
- **A regulated subject.** Health, finance, legal, and children's topics carry claim rules the platform enforces regardless of the news peg.
- **A short fuse.** A topic that will be resolved or reversed within a day, where the post may age badly by the time it publishes.

State the risk, offer the angles that do not carry it, and proceed with whatever the user picks. This is their call — the job here is to make sure they make it knowingly rather than to make it for them.

## Facts about real events

Whatever the post asserts about the topic comes from the user, or from a lookup this package ran and attributed with its read time, per [looking up what is trending](trend-lookup.md). Those are the only two sources.

Dates, figures, names, outcomes, official statements, and quotes are the user's to supply. The trend's own state — how much momentum it has, whether it is peaking or fading, how long it has left, and who is talking about it — comes from either source, and a lookup carries the time it was read.

A board position or a post count is a fact. Peaking, fading, and shelf life remain inference drawn from it, offered as inference with the evidence beside them. Never estimate any of it from your own priors; say the shelf life is unstated rather than guessing it. When one is missing, write the post around it — an angle can carry a reaction, a use, or an opinion without restating the details. Never produce a plausible-looking detail about a real event; a wrong fact about something people are actively discussing is the fastest way to make the post the story.
